# SAI Performance Analyst (Physiotherapy): source research for a 2,000-question bank

Research completed: 12 August 2026 (Asia/Kolkata)

Scope: publicly available SAI Performance Analyst / Physiotherapist recruitment material, mock or previous-paper claims, syllabus-linked study material, and high-trust sources suitable for authoring original questions. This report is for content research only; it does not alter the application or its question bank.

## Executive findings

1. I found official SAI recruitment advertisements, job descriptions, archives, shortlists and results, but I did not find a verifiable official written Performance Analyst (Physiotherapy) question paper or answer key from 2020–2026.
2. The 2022 and 2023 High Performance Analyst material is recruitment material for contractual appointments. The official 2022 result explicitly describes interviews, and the SAI archive contains a 2023 “shortlisted for interview” record. These are useful for role scope and competency framing, not as evidence of a previous CBT paper.
3. The 2026 regular-recruitment notification supplied in this repository is the controlling exam source. It contains the CBT structure, discipline syllabus and illustrative Annexure B questions. Those examples should be used to understand style and depth only; questions must not be copied or lightly paraphrased.
4. Commercial sites advertise SAI-specific mock tests and question banks, but the pages reviewed do not establish official provenance. One provider’s previous-paper page explicitly says that no previous-paper package is currently available. These pages are pattern leads only and should not be treated as past SAI papers.
5. The strongest defensible authoring base is a combination of the SAI syllabus and sample style, SAI/Khelo India operational material, current WADA/IOC/ACSM guidance, freely accessible consensus statements and clinical practice guidelines, and the standard textbooks named in Annexure B.

## Authoring and copyright rules

1. Write original stems, options and explanations. Do not copy any question, option order, explanation, table, case passage or distinctive wording from the SAI annexures, commercial mock pages, textbooks or journal articles.
2. Use sources to identify claims, thresholds, decision rules and clinical reasoning pathways. Then construct a new athlete, sport, dataset and decision problem. A source citation does not make copied wording permissible.
3. Prefer one defensible best answer. Exclude questions where accepted practice is genuinely disputed, the answer depends on an unavailable local protocol, or two options could reasonably be correct.
4. Keep source metadata internal and granular: `sourceId`, bibliographic title, URL/DOI, publication or revision date, access date, edition, claim supported, and `asOf` for time-sensitive content.
5. Distinguish guideline recommendations from textbook conventions and from emerging evidence. The learner-facing explanation should state the principle and its limitation rather than overclaim certainty.
6. For A2, separate stable facts from current affairs. Use official SAI, Ministry, IOC/Olympics, WADA and federation pages, capture the publication/update date, and maintain a review queue.
7. Before release, require two-pass review: clinical/content review by a qualified physiotherapist or sports-medicine reviewer, then independent answer-key review. Automated duplicate/count checks are necessary but cannot establish clinical quality.

## Recommended bank production sequence

1. Freeze the 2026 notification blueprint and the source manifest.
2. Author A1 and B in topic batches, with the largest allocations to sports physiotherapy, assessment/clinical reasoning, exercise therapy/rehabilitation, biomechanics, anatomy and athlete monitoring.
3. Author approximately 100 Section C passages with four linked questions each. Keep the entire passage and its four questions together in source files.
4. Add A2 in dated batches, with an explicit coverage label such as “sports awareness through August 2026”.
5. Run structural validation, duplicate detection, source-link checks and answer-key checks before each release.
6. Have a subject-matter reviewer reject ambiguous, outdated, unsupported or overly textbook-trivial questions before calling the bank “verified”.

This research supports authoring 2,000 individually written questions, but it does not support claiming that any publicly available SAI previous-paper archive was found. The final bank should therefore be described as an original, syllabus-aligned preparation bank informed by official and authoritative sources—not as reproduced SAI papers.

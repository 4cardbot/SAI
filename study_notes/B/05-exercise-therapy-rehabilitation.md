# B — Exercise Therapy & Rehabilitation

## Rehabilitation staircase

```text
Protect / calm symptoms → restore range of motion and activation → build capacity
→ integrate proprioception/strength → power/speed/change of direction
→ sport-specific practice → unrestricted performance exposure
```

Progress by **response and criteria**, not by calendar alone.

## Exercise principles

**Specificity · overload · progression · individualisation · reversibility · variation · recovery.**

For every exercise specify: position, load, range, speed, sets/repetitions, rest, pain response, quality criterion and progression/regression.

## Contraction and loading

| Mode | Useful cue |
| --- | --- |
| Isometric | Tension at a fixed joint angle; useful when movement is irritable or for activation, but angle-specific |
| Concentric | Muscle shortens while producing force |
| Eccentric | Muscle lengthens under load; high force and tissue-loading stimulus; dose progressively |
| Isokinetic | Constant angular speed with accommodating resistance; specialised equipment |

For tendinopathy, progressive tendon loading may include isometrics, isotonic/eccentric or heavy-slow resistance and sport exposure; the best choice depends on stage, symptoms and capacity.

## Flexibility and proprioceptive neuromuscular facilitation

| Method | Main cue |
| --- | --- |
| Static | Held position; low ballistic demand |
| Dynamic | Controlled movement through range; useful in preparation |
| Ballistic | Bouncing/rapid end-range; higher demand and context-sensitive |
| Hold–relax | Isometric contraction followed by relaxation and increased range |
| Contract–relax | Concentric/isotonic contraction followed by relaxation and increased range |
| Agonist contraction | Antagonist relaxation facilitated by active agonist movement |

## Open versus closed chain

```text
Open: distal segment free; isolate or bias a muscle/joint angle
Closed: distal segment fixed; co-contraction and distributed loading
```

Choose based on tissue protection, joint shear/compression, task demand and athlete tolerance—not labels alone.

## Maitland grades

```text
I  small amplitude, beginning of range        pain modulation
II large amplitude, within available range    pain / movement tolerance
III large amplitude, into resistance          mobility
IV small amplitude, at end range               mobility
V  small, fast thrust at end range             manipulation by trained clinician
```

The grade is not the whole treatment: direction, examination findings, irritability, contraindications and reassessment decide appropriateness.

## Proprioception, plyometrics, hydrotherapy

- Proprioception: change sensory and motor-control demand—stable → unstable, predictable → perturbation, bilateral → unilateral, slow → fast.
- Plyometrics: eccentric braking followed by rapid concentric action; require adequate strength, landing control and tissue readiness.
- Aquatic therapy unloads body weight and changes resistance; water depth, buoyancy, drag and temperature alter the dose.

## Easy ways to remember it

- Progress in this order: **restore movement, restore activation, build strength, then practise the sport task**.
- Maitland grades I and II are usually used for gentle pain or movement tolerance; grades III and IV emphasise mobility; grade V is a thrust performed by an appropriately trained clinician.
- Increase difficulty step by step: **range → load → speed → complexity → unpredictable sport demands**.

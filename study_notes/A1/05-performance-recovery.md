# A1 — Performance Enhancement & Recovery Protocols

## Programme logic

```text
Goal → demand analysis → baseline → dose (FITT) → recover
     → monitor response → progress/regress → sport-specific exposure
```

**FITT:** Frequency, Intensity, Time, Type. Add volume, density, complexity and velocity when appropriate.

## Training principles

| Principle | Exam meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Specificity | Adaptation follows the energy system, movement, velocity and decision demand trained |
| Overload | Stimulus exceeds the athlete’s current habitual demand |
| Progression | Increase challenge gradually and deliberately |
| Individualisation | Dose depends on athlete, sport, position, history and readiness |
| Variation/periodisation | Organise stress and recovery across time; avoid random change |
| Reversibility | Unused adaptations decline with detraining |
| Recovery | Adaptation is built between exposures, not during unbroken stress |

## Strength versus power

```text
Strength = force capacity
Power = force × velocity
```

An athlete can be strong but slow. To improve power, preserve force while training intent and movement velocity; select loads and exercises that fit the task.

## Recovery hierarchy

```text
1. Fix the dose: training, competition and travel load
2. Sleep and circadian routine
3. Fuel and hydration
4. Active recovery / mobility / psychological down-regulation
5. Targeted modalities when a clear purpose and tolerable evidence exist
```

| Tool | Use carefully |
| --- | --- |
| Active recovery | Low-intensity movement may reduce perceived stiffness; it is not a cure for excessive load |
| Cryotherapy/heat | Context-specific effects; do not use a modality to mask danger or replace diagnosis |
| Compression | May help symptoms/recovery perception in some settings; individual response varies |
| Massage | Short-term symptoms/relaxation; does not replace progressive loading |
| Sleep | Protect duration, regularity, dark/cool room and pre-sleep routine; individual need varies |

## Injury-risk lens

```text
External load + internal response + tissue capacity + recovery + context
                                  ↓
                         risk / readiness decision
```

Progress load by response and criteria, not calendar alone. Watch spikes, monotonous training, sudden high-speed exposure, illness, low energy availability and poor sleep.

## Nutrition and supplements

- Carbohydrate: high-intensity training and glycogen restoration.
- Protein: distribute adequate high-quality protein across the day; increase attention during immobilisation/rehabilitation.
- Hydration: individualise to sweat rate, climate, duration and sodium losses; avoid both large dehydration and overdrinking.
- Supplement rule: **food first → evidence → contamination risk → athlete-specific need → third-party testing**. No supplement is automatically safe because it is “natural.”

## Memory tactics

- **SOPR:** Specificity, Overload, Progression, Recovery.
- Recovery priority: **Dose–Sleep–Fuel–Tools**.
- Power formula: **F × V**; if velocity is absent from the plan, it is not a complete power plan.

## Self-test

1. Why is progressive loading preferable to an arbitrary “week 6 clearance”?
2. Distinguish strength from power in one sentence.
3. Name the first three recovery levers before passive modalities.
4. Why is a supplement a risk-management question as well as a performance question?
